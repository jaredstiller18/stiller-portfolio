1 of 7 — Tan Lab, CU-Boulder

Closed-loop Hypoxic Air Delivery and Control System

Project Objective

Build a closed-loop rig that automatically holds a subject's blood oxygen saturation in a target band by switching between hypoxic and normoxic air — for altitude-training and physiology research use, not a commercial kit.

Process & Skills

  • Gas path: Hypoxico HYP-123 PSA generator (hypoxic) plus a room-air blower (normoxic) as dual sources
  • Feedback: Masimo Root pulse oximeter streamed over USB serial at 921,600 baud — SpO2 / BPM / PI / RR at 1 Hz
  • Actuation: 4x 2-way solenoid valves driven by a SainSmart FT245R relay board in async bitbang mode via ftd2xx
  • Fail-safe: de-energized state defaults to normoxic air by design
  • Software: Python/Dash dashboard — live vitals plotting, a 15-episode protocol, manual valve override, CSV logging
  • Iterated the controller from a reactive threshold design to a predictive-pulse strategy after characterizing system lag

Results

  • Across 18 tuning sessions, refined the controller to hold SpO2 tightly in the 79–83% band through the second half of each session
  • Measured real desaturation/resaturation dynamics (~0.38%/s vs. up to 5%/s) and used them to eliminate an early 10–14% recovery overshoot
  • Passive fail-safe held throughout testing — every relay or software fault defaulted the subject back to normal air
PID control Solenoid/relay driving Serial protocol integration Safety-first design

2 of 7 — Tan Lab, CU-Boulder

nRF52 BLE Research Instrumentation Platform

Project Objective

Unified three separate pieces of physiology research hardware: a NIRS monitor, a pneumatic cuff inflator, and an electrical stimulator, under one BLE bridge and web dashboard. Allowed the lab to run a single sequenced protocol with shared timestamps, instead of juggling three standalone instruments.

Process & Skills

  • Circuit design: Designed the relay driver circuit and stimulator trigger circuit to control cuff inflation and e-stim via a Nordic's GPIOs.
  • Administrative: Formatted a BOM that was approved, ordered parts, and filed appropriate paperwork (tax-refunds). Later produced a Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) and System and Architecure Documentation.
  • Hardware construction: Hand-built and soldered the circuits above, placing them into a working enclosure with USB-Micro-C output to computer, BNC output to the e-stim, and 1/4" Audio Jack to the cuff inflator.
  • Embedded firmware: Wrote the BLE bridge in Zephyr RTOS (C89, NCS v2.0.0) using the Nordic nRF52-DK, for BT-streaming from a Moxy NIRS monitor.
  • Protocol engineering: Implemented the specified research protocol for cuff inflation and precise 5V TTL triggering (~10 ms) for the DS7R stimulator.
  • Full-stack dashboard: Built the live monitoring and protocol-runner interface in vanilla JS / Chart.js / Tailwind, with the entire protocol available to run with one button. Also added contingency manual controls for the cuff inflator and e-stim and a button to manually save data, although automatically backed up on the hard-drive.
Zephyr / C firmware BLE Analog driver design Protocol sequencing

5 of 7 — RE project

Reverse engineering: a manual coffee grinder

Project Objective

Reverse-engineer a manual coffee grinder for a mechanical design course — understand why it's built the way it is, then propose a genuinely improved version.

Process & Skills

  • Fully disassembled the grinder and mapped its product architecture
  • Built black-box, glass-box, and fishbone diagrams; scored the design for DFA (part count, assembly complexity)
  • Compared materials with Ashby charts (strength/density, wear/hardness, fracture toughness) across its plastic, ceramic, and metal components

Results

  • Proposed consolidating the cup-holder assembly and snap-fitting the plastic ring to cut part count
  • Substituted Aluminum 6061 for a stainless component, backed by a revised cost and DFA analysis
DFA / DFM Material selection Mechanical design Cost analysis

6 of 7 — Temperature sensors

PMMA barrel temperature monitoring system

Project Objective

Build a temperature-monitoring subsystem for a full-scale mockup of the DarkSide-20K dark matter detector's cryogenic core (Williams College honors thesis), to protect its PMMA structure from thermal-shock cracking during cooldown.

Process & Skills

  • Instrumented 44 RTD sensors across panels and depths of the PMMA barrel
  • Designed a custom 3-wire resistance readout into ADCs for each sensor
  • Built a live Grafana dashboard for real-time thermal-gradient monitoring during cooling

Results

  • Sensor readout held within 1°C agreement across the full 44-sensor array during live cryostat cooldown — enough precision to inform a controlled cooling protocol
  • Part of the thesis work recognized with Sigma-Xi honors in physics
RTD/ADC design Cryogenic instrumentation Thermal monitoring

7 of 7 — Level meter

Short level meter system

Project Objective

Build a capacitive level-sensing subsystem for the same DarkSide-20K mockup, keeping the liquid-argon interface level and correctly aligned with the detector's internal components.

Process & Skills

  • Built a custom capacitance-to-depth curve-fit model from calibration data

Results

  • Part of the thesis work recognized with Sigma-Xi honors in physics (Williams College)

ADDITIONAL COURSEWORK

Robotics, controls & biomechanics coursework

Two more team projects, kept out of the main seven above but still hands-on.

Coursework

Robotics & controls: four challenge problems

Project Objective

Four independent challenge problems for Intro to Robotics (CU Boulder), progressing from discrete pathfinding to continuous control, each a self-contained simulation with its own write-up.

Process & Skills

  • CP1 — Multi-robot pathfinding: conflict-resolution algorithms routing robots across a shared grid without collisions
  • CP2 — RRT motion planning: a non-holonomic Rapidly-Exploring Random Tree planner for a TurtleBot in PyBullet
  • CP3 — Inverse kinematics: drove a 7-DOF Franka Emika Panda arm to render images and lettering on a virtual blackboard
  • CP4 — Inverted-pendulum control: derived the cart-pendulum equations of motion via Lagrangian mechanics, then designed and tuned a PD controller

Results

  • CP1: benchmarked two collision-avoidance strategies over 100-run trials
  • CP2: achieved a 90% goal-reach rate across 100 randomized trials
  • CP4: stabilized the inverted pendulum and produced a clean step response with the tuned PD controller
Control theory Motion planning Inverse kinematics Python / PyBullet

Coursework

Knee rotation asymmetry in ACL injury risk

Project Objective

Reanalyze public motion-capture data to test whether leg dominance affects knee-rotation patterns during a landing task linked to non-contact ACL injury risk.

Process & Skills

  • Reanalyzed 3D mocap + force-plate data from a 44-participant jump-landing study (Vicon system, 59 markers, pre/post fatigue)
  • Recomputed joint-angle time series via OpenSim inverse kinematics, isolating the single-leg medial-rotation hop
  • Grounded the comparison in the sports-medicine literature on leg-dominance asymmetry as an ACL risk factor

Results

  • Statistically compared internal/external knee rotation between dominant and non-dominant legs during the landing window
OpenSim / IK Motion capture analysis Biomechanics Statistical comparison
