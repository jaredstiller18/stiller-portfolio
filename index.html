Precision instrumentation for the DarkSide-20K dark matter detector

Honors thesis in physics (Williams College). Designed and deployed two sensing subsystems on a full-scale mockup of a dark matter detector's cryogenic core, benchmarking them ahead of use in the real experiment.

  • Short level meter: 4 capacitive sensors around the TPC circumference, sub-millimeter precision, sensitive to as little as 0.01° of tilt in the liquid-argon interface
  • Temperature monitoring: 44 RTD sensors across the PMMA barrel panels, tracking thermal gradients to prevent thermal-shock cracking during cryostat cooling
  • Readout chain: custom 3-wire resistance measurement into ADCs, with a live Grafana dashboard for both sensor networks
  • Validation: built a custom stainless-steel test fixture with liquid nitrogen as a LAr surrogate to correlate capacitance against known depth
  • Sensor readout stayed within 1°C agreement across the array during live cryostat cooling and filling
Capacitive sensing RTD/ADC design Cryogenic instrumentation Data modeling

Closed-loop hypoxic air delivery system

A PID-controlled altitude training rig that regulates blood oxygen saturation in real time by switching between hypoxic and normoxic air streams — built for a research/training use case, not a commercial kit.

  • Gas source: Hypoxico HYP-123 PSA generator plus a room-air blower for the normoxic stream
  • Feedback: Masimo Root pulse oximeter over USB serial at 921,600 baud, streaming SpO2 / BPM / PI / RR at 1 Hz
  • Actuation: 4x 2-way solenoid valves (2 NC / 2 NO) driven by a SainSmart FT245R relay board in async bitbang mode via ftd2xx
  • Fail-safe: de-energized state passively routes normoxic air by design — the rig can't fail into a hypoxic state
  • Software: Python/Dash dashboard with live vitals plotting, a 15-episode treatment protocol, per-valve manual override, and CSV logging
PID control Solenoid/relay driving Serial protocol integration Safety-first design

nRF52 BLE research instrumentation platform

A wireless bridge that unifies three pieces of research hardware — a NIRS monitor, a pneumatic cuff inflator, and an electrical stimulator — under one custom firmware stack and web dashboard, for running sequenced physiology protocols.

  • Bridge hardware: Nordic nRF52-DK running Zephyr RTOS (C89, NCS v2.0.0) as a BLE central
  • Sensing: streams data from a Moxy near-infrared spectroscopy monitor over BLE
  • Cuff control: E20 Rapid Cuff Inflator switched through a JZC-11F relay with a 2N3904 transistor driver stage
  • Stimulation: DS7R constant-current stimulator triggered with a 5V TTL pulse at ~10 ms width, brought out to a BNC jack
  • Dashboard: vanilla JS / Chart.js / Tailwind front end for live monitoring and running a fixed experimental sequence — baseline, preconditioning, stimulation, rest
Zephyr / C firmware BLE Analog driver design Protocol sequencing

Robotics & controls: four challenge problems

A semester of team challenge problems for Intro to Robotics (CU Boulder), moving from discrete pathfinding to continuous control — each one a self-contained simulation with its own write-up and results.

  • CP1 — Multi-robot pathfinding: conflict-resolution algorithms routing robots across a shared grid without collisions; benchmarked two decision strategies over 100-run trials
  • CP2 — RRT motion planning: a non-holonomic Rapidly-Exploring Random Tree planner for a TurtleBot in PyBullet, 90% goal-reach rate over 100 randomized trials
  • CP3 — Inverse kinematics: drove a 7-DOF Franka Emika Panda arm to render images and lettering on a virtual blackboard via IK-based point-to-point motion
  • CP4 — Inverted-pendulum control: derived the cart-pendulum equations of motion via Lagrangian mechanics, then designed and tuned a PD controller to stabilize and step the system
Control theory Motion planning Inverse kinematics Python / PyBullet

Reverse engineering: a manual coffee grinder

A full teardown-to-redesign study of a consumer product for a mechanical design course — disassembly, functional analysis, material selection, and a concrete redesign proposal.

Knee rotation asymmetry in ACL injury risk

A biomechanics reanalysis of public motion-capture data, testing whether dominant vs. non-dominant legs show different knee rotation patterns during a high-risk landing task linked to non-contact ACL injury.

  • Data: reanalyzed 3D mocap + force-plate data from a 44-participant jump-landing study (Vicon system, 59 markers, pre/post fatigue)
  • Pipeline: joint angle time series recomputed via OpenSim inverse kinematics, isolating the single-leg medial rotation hop
  • Question: compared internal/external knee rotation between dominant and non-dominant legs during the landing window
  • Framing: grounded the analysis in the sports-medicine literature on leg-dominance asymmetry as an ACL injury risk factor
OpenSim / IK Motion capture analysis Biomechanics Statistical comparison
